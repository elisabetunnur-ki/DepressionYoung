Phenome-wide Association Study Results

Cox regression was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) for associations between early-life depression and subsequent medical conditions. Highlighted results are statistically significant positive associations (HR > 1 and q-value < 0.05), where the q-value is the false discovery rate–adjusted p-value.

Participants with prespecified pre-existing psychiatric or serious somatic conditions were excluded, and a 6-month lag was applied after the index date. PheWAS analyses included conditions recorded in more than 0.5% of individuals with depression in the corresponding analysis. Diagnoses were grouped into clinically related Phecodes.

In the population-based analyses, Cox models were conditioned on the matching variables and adjusted for birth region and parental education and income during childhood. In the sibling-based analyses, models were conditioned on sibship and adjusted for sex when applicable, age at the index date, and birth order.

Diagnosis-age-stratified population analyses are shown for depression diagnosed before age 25 years and at ages 25–40 years. These labels refer to age at the first recorded specialist depression diagnosis, not disease onset.

The interactive plots allow all available HR estimates to be viewed. The manuscript PheWAS figures show only conditions meeting the predefined significance criterion.
